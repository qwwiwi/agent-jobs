- Prune removes a finished job's temp dir (`<job dir>/tmp`) 2 hours after the job ended instead of with the folder after 48 hours: new config `job_tmp_retention_hours` (default 2, `0` = as before), applied by the existing prune path and checks, and the daemon runs that temp dir pass every 10 minutes (#240).
- `system_doctor` totals all job folders in `disk.job_dirs_bytes` (running jobs measured live by a bounded walk, `disk.job_dirs_partial` when it is a lower bound) and warns from the new config `job_dirs_warn_gb` (default 20, `0` = never) (#240).
- Prune never crosses a mount point: every directory is opened with `openat2` (`RESOLVE_NO_XDEV|RESOLVE_BENEATH`) and checked against the device the removal started on, so a filesystem mounted inside a job folder (a bind mount included) is neither entered nor removed, its siblings still go and the job reports the path. Where `openat2` is unavailable (Linux before 5.6, a seccomp profile) nothing is removed. This applies to the 48 h prune, the 2 h temp dir pass, archive expiry and the sources and manifest cleanups; one removal is bounded to 60 s and continues on the next pass (#240).
- Prune leaves a finished job alone while its cgroup scope stop is still pending: cleanup, resume and prune now share one hold rule (pending scope stop, pending advisor calls, live process groups) (#240).
- `system_doctor` no longer runs a prune dry run under the lifecycle lock: `disk.prunable_bytes` is an estimate from recorded sizes, the new `disk.prune_overdue` counts finished jobs whose temp dir is still there well past the retention (listed as a problem with the oldest job id), and a recorded partial size of a finished job now sets `disk.job_dirs_partial`. `agent-jobs prune --dry-run` measures with a bounded walk and marks a capped figure `partial` ("at least") (#240).
